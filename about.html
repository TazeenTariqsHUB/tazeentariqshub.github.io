Why Not Both?

Hi, I'm Tazeen Tariq — a Cybersecurity student, a QA Engineer, and a fashion enthusiast. People ask me, "If fashion, then why tech?" My answer has always been the same: Why not?

I've never started something without seeing it through to my best — a research job, a QA role, cybersecurity, fashion, it doesn't matter which. The dedication doesn't change depending on how easy or hard the thing is. I've pushed to keep my GPA where I want it, but grades were never the whole story for me — I've made a point of getting out into the real world too: building a wide network, meeting people, learning to read a room. And I'm quick to pick things up, especially hands-on — give me something to actually build and I'll learn it faster than I'd ever learn it from a slide. That's how Aqua Orbit Sentinel happened: a shark-tracking system I built for the NASA Space Apps Challenge that placed 4th regionally, in a field (object detection) I hadn't touched before I started it.

As a child I struggled in, and even failed, multiple subjects — except computer studies, which came to me almost naturally. That contrast taught me early that effort and aptitude are two different things, and that I could choose which subjects got which. I chose to put real effort into technology and, later, fashion, and treat both like they deserved my best.

In cybersecurity specifically, I like that the smallest mistake can create a real gap with real consequences. That's not something to fear — it's a reason to be more precise, more careful, and more thorough than the work strictly requires. That same instinct is why I went and wrote "OWASP Top 10 for Software Engineers": if I understand something well enough to rely on it, I want to be able to explain it well enough that someone else can too.

I've also learned to lead, not just execute — I've served as President of the Media Society at two different institutions, competed and won as a public speaker, and taught O-Level Mathematics while I was still a student myself. Leading people, teaching people, and speaking in front of them taught me things no textbook did.

I'm genuinely curious by nature — I like expanding what I know even when nothing requires it of me, and I bring a creative streak to things that could otherwise stay purely technical. Whatever guidance I didn't have along the way, I made up for by figuring it out myself and giving it everything I had.

Technology and fashion look like two different worlds from the outside. To me they're the same thing: perfection, creativity, precision, and the willingness to challenge myself. So if someone asks me again, "If fashion, then why tech?"

I'll still say: Why not both?
